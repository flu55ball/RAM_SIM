# 06 user testing and feedback

The full feedback document can be viewed here:

[View full feedback PDF](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1Z-IYGBA_kUadek652xmqTZt3ulfJvE8O/view?usp=sharing)

This page is my own reflection on the feedback graphs. Rather than breaking down every written answer, I looked broadly at the before and after results to see what had improved and what still needed work.

## General note

* Some questions were useful as context but did not really affect the design decisions.
* For example, how familiar people were with VR games does not matter too much here.
* How familiar people were with computers, PC hardware, or RAM installation also does not matter too much, because the game should still be understandable to a first-time player.
* The more useful feedback came from questions about objective clarity, movement, grabbing, puzzle feedback, labels, sound, and overall concept.

## Did the game's main objective feel clear at the start?

* This improved compared to the first version.
* Before, 75% said the objective was only somewhat clear, and 25% said no.
* After the changes, 60% said yes, 20% said somewhat, and 20% said no.
* So things looked a little better.
* The objective was clearer than before, but not perfect.
* This still shows that the game needs better onboarding and clearer instructions at the start.

## Would a short tutorial or controls guide be helpful?

* People generally said yes, a short tutorial or controls guide would be helpful.
* This was after them seeing the little guide PDF or instruction PDF that I created.
* So even with an instruction document, it still seems useful to have clear guidance.
* This supports adding a proper controls screen or goal screen inside the game.

## How easy was it to move around the space?

* Everyone found it easy, or easy enough, to move around.
* They found it easier after the improvements.
* There was some sort of percentage improvement compared to the first round.
* This suggests the movement setup was going in the right direction.

## How did the walking speed feel?

* The walking speed feedback changed a bit.
* It went from a general mix of opinions to people mostly saying it was about right, or even slightly too slow.
* I did increase the walk speed a little bit.
* The result seems better overall, even if it may still feel slightly slow to some players.
* For this game, slightly slow is not necessarily a huge issue because the player is mostly working in a small area.

## How did the player height / camera height feel?

* People felt taller after the changes.
* Some people almost felt too tall compared to before.
* This was better overall, because before the player height felt too low.
* The height probably improved, but it may still need a little bit of balancing.

## How easy was it to pick up and place the RAM sticks?

* This was one of the clearest improvements.
* There was basically a 100% increase in how easy people found it.
* Everyone agreed that the RAM sticks were much easier to pick up this round.
* This shows that the grab setup, attach points, and interaction changes worked.

## Did the RAM pieces ever feel too close to your face / camera while holding them?

* Everyone said no after I fixed it.
* So this issue seems to have been fixed.
* This was an important improvement because the RAM previously felt too close to the camera.
* Fixing this made the interaction feel less awkward.

## Did the grab distance feel correct?

* Everyone said yes after I redid it.
* This was an improvement compared to before, where some people said no.
* The grab distance now seems to feel correct.
* This is important because the RAM interaction is the main physical action in the game.

## Did the snap turning / rotation feel comfortable?

* 100% said yes.
* This suggests the rotation setup was comfortable.
* I do not think this needs much more work unless another tester specifically mentions it.

## How easy was it to understand what the lights and feedback meant?

* There was an increase in people understanding the lights and feedback more.
* However, there were still some mixed reactions.
* This means the feedback improved, but it is still not completely clear.
* The lights are important because they are how the player understands whether the computer is working or failing.
* This is still an area that needs more polish.

## Were the labels / indicators clear enough?

* This improved a lot.
* It went from 100% saying no, to around 60% saying yes.
* That is a big improvement.
* However, it also means the labels and indicators still are not fully solved.
* The labels probably need to be bigger, clearer, or explained better.

## How easy was it to understand the puzzle rules?

* People understood it more than before.
* There was still one person who found it quite difficult to understand.
* This shows that the puzzle logic is more readable now, but still not obvious enough for everyone.
* The game probably needs clearer stage feedback so players know when they have completed part of the puzzle.

## How did the puzzle difficulty feel?

* People thought it felt easier than before.
* However, it was still quite difficult.
* I think this is not necessarily a bad thing, because the puzzle is meant to be a bit awkward and frustrating.
* The main issue is not just difficulty, it is whether the player understands why they failed.

## Did the puzzle feel satisfying or just frustrating?

* People thought it felt more satisfying after the changes.
* The sound being a little bit louder helped.
* The RAM clicks and button sounds made the actions feel more satisfying.
* This supports the decision to focus on sound feedback and physical interaction.

## Did the sequence / pacing of the puzzle feel too slow, too fast, or about right?

* Everyone generally thought the pacing was a bit slow.
* That is kind of the purpose of it.
* It was supposed to be slow and frustrating.
* The important thing is making sure the frustration feels intentional and funny, rather than confusing or broken.

## How noticeable were the sound effects during interaction?

* People said the sound effects were very noticeable.
* Most people agreed they were easy to hear.
* Around 80% to 100% of the feedback suggested the sound was very clear.
* This is a good result because sound feedback is one of the main parts of the game.

## Did the sound feedback make your actions feel more satisfying?

* 100% said yes.
* This is one of the strongest results from the feedback.
* It shows that the sound design is working.
* The recorded RAM sounds and randomised button sounds helped make the game feel more physical and satisfying.

## Was the game's feedback clear when something was correct or incorrect?

* 60% said yes, but 40% said no.
* This means the feedback is better, but still not clear enough.
* The player needs to understand when something is correct or incorrect.
* This is probably one of the main things to improve next.
* The game needs clearer feedback when a stage is completed or when the RAM setup is wrong.

## How interesting did you find the overall concept?

* 60% said it was very interesting.
* The remaining responses were still fairly positive, with people giving it a 3 or 4 rating.
* This suggests that the overall concept works.
* The idea of a strange VR RAM repair puzzle is interesting enough to keep developing.

## Did the game world / environment feel too big, too small, or about right?

* 60% said the game world felt about right.
* 40% said it felt slightly too small.
* This is better than the earlier feedback where the space felt too big.
* The smaller, more focused space seems to work better for the puzzle.
* Slightly too small is probably not a major issue because the game is meant to focus on the motherboard and RAM interaction.

## Overall reflection

* The second round of feedback showed that the game improved.
* The biggest improvements were:
  * the RAM sticks were much easier to pick up
  * the RAM no longer felt too close to the camera
  * the grab distance felt correct
  * snap turning felt comfortable
  * the objective was clearer
  * the labels and indicators improved
  * the sound feedback made the interaction more satisfying
* The areas that still need work are:
  * clearer puzzle rules
  * clearer stage feedback
  * clearer lights and indicators
  * clearer feedback when something is correct or incorrect
* Overall, the feedback suggests that the core idea works.
* The game is still a bit slow and frustrating, but that is partly the point.
* The next improvement is making sure the frustration feels intentional rather than confusing.
